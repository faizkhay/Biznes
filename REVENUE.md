# How biznes.mu makes money

Written as the site's revenue manager, 20 November 2026, for the owner. Nothing here is built into
the site or promised on it. Every market figure names its source; every price of ours is a
proposal to test, and every forecast says what it assumes. The facts behind it are in
`content/market.json`, each with the page it was read on.

## The short answer

The site earns in four ways, in this order of how soon and how surely:

1. **Khay Digital's own work.** About 6,500 of the businesses listed have no website on record. The
   directory is the best prospect list the agency has ever had, and it costs nothing to use.
2. **Introductions to founders and investors.** People reading "incorporate a company" or "invest
   from abroad" are about to buy services worth thousands of dollars. Firms will pay to meet them.
3. **Paid profiles.** A business claims its listing free, and pays a small yearly fee to verify and
   enrich it.
4. **Sponsored places, clearly marked.** One labelled slot above a category or a town.

Data licences, a subscription for accountants and sponsored guides come later.

One rule protects all of it: **the order of results is never for sale.** The day a visitor suspects
the first pharmacy is first because it paid, the site is one more directory. Everything below is
sold beside the results, with a label, and never inside them.

## Where we stand today

- **What we own:** 7,179 listings in 129 towns and villages, 13 guides with 334 official sources
  between them, a glossary, four tools, and a crawler that rebuilds all of it on demand.
- **What we do not have yet:** visitors. The site is not launched and has no traffic, so every
  number in the forecast below is an assumption, not a measurement. Revenue follows audience, and
  the first job is to earn it.
- **What the listings lack:** 6,496 of the 6,819 listings from the map have no website on record
  and 6,308 no telephone number. That is the gap a claimed profile fills, and the opening for the
  agency.

## The streams

### 1. Khay Digital's own work: first, and free to start

**Who pays:** a listed business with no website, a thin one, or no brand to speak of.

**What they buy:** a website, a brand, a claimed and filled profile, from the agency that runs the
directory.

**Why it comes first:** it needs no audience, no payment system and no new product. The index
already says which businesses have no site, in which town, in which trade. A filter and a telephone
turn that into a call list: every hotel and guest house in Grand Baie with no website, every
hardware shop in Flacq.

**The sum:** 6,496 listings have no website on record. If one in two hundred becomes a client in a
year, that is 32 projects. At MUR 60,000 each, which sits inside the published MUR 25,000 to MUR
250,000 band, that is about MUR 1.9 million. Both the rate and the price are assumptions.

**What to build:** a private view of the index for the agency (by trade, by town, with or without a
site). On the site, one line on a listing with no website: "Is this your business? Get it a
website", leading to the form that already exists.

**The care it needs:** absence from the map is not proof a business has no site. Ask before
assuming. And contact businesses by the telephone numbers they publish, never by harvested email:
the index holds none, by design.

### 2. Introductions: the most valuable visitors we will have

**Who pays:** company secretaries, accountants, law firms, management companies, banks, insurers,
trade mark agents, immigration advisers.

**What they buy:** a request from a person who has said what they need. A reader of "Incorporate a
company" presses "Ask three firms for a quote", answers four questions, and the request goes to up
to three firms that have agreed terms with us.

**Why it is worth the most:** the service being bought costs from about USD 1,000 for a permit
application to USD 2,400 a year for a company secretary, every year. A firm that keeps a client
five years earns five figures in dollars. Paying for the introduction is ordinary business for
them.

**Price to test:** MUR 1,500 to MUR 3,000 for a domestic request (a company, an accountant, a trade
mark), and USD 100 to USD 250 for an investor's or a global business request. Or a monthly fee per
firm per subject, once volume is steady. Start per request: it is the easiest yes.

**The size:** 24,571 enterprises were registered in 2025, about 2,000 a month, and 10,550
occupation permits were applied for. Twenty requests a month would be 1% of new registrations.

**What to build:** the request form on the relevant guides and tools, a short list of partner
firms with written terms, a way to send and count requests, and a monthly statement.

**The care it needs:** the guide's text never recommends a firm and never changes for one. The
request box is labelled as a paid service to firms. Partners are checked against the registers the
site itself lists. A reader's details go only to the firms they chose to ask.

### 3. Paid profiles: small, steady, and the base of everything else

**Who pays:** the business that owns a listing.

**Free, always:** claiming the listing, correcting it, removing it. A directory that charges to fix
its own mistakes earns contempt.

**Paid:** a verified profile. We check the business against the companies register, mark it
"Verified", and let it add what the open map cannot hold: a description, photographs, a WhatsApp
button, a menu or a price list, a note for public holidays, and a monthly count of how many people
saw the listing and pressed "Call".

**Price to test:** MUR 400 a month, billed as MUR 4,800 a year. For comparison, Yelo's premium
listing is USD 65 a year and the chamber's smallest membership is MUR 8,000. The verification and
the call count are what justify the price. A second tier for a business with several branches can
follow.

**The size:** if 400 businesses claim in the first year and 15% of them pay, that is 60 profiles
and MUR 288,000. Small. But a claimed listing is a business we can speak to, and every later
stream sells to that list.

**What to build:** accounts, the claim flow with a check that the claimant is the business, the
profile editor, a payment route (a local card gateway or bank transfer to begin with), and counts
of views and calls.

**The care it needs:** "Verified" must mean one thing, said on the page: that we matched the
business to its entry in the companies register on a given day. A paid profile is fuller, so it
sorts higher among equals, exactly as any fuller listing does today. It never jumps the order.

### 4. Sponsored places: only once there is traffic to sell

**Who pays:** a business that wants to be seen for one trade in one town or across the island.

**What they buy:** the single slot above a category or a town page, marked "Sponsored", with its
own background. One sponsor a page. The organic list beneath is untouched.

**Price to test:** MUR 1,500 to MUR 5,000 a month by category and reach. For scale: in 2018 a
banner on one section of the country's largest news site was MUR 3,000 to MUR 4,500 a week, with
1.2 million readers a month behind it. We will be far smaller at first, so we start low and sell
by the result: the sponsor sees their views and calls.

**The size:** 70 categories and 273 towns and neighbourhoods make a large inventory, but only a few dozen pages will
have enough visitors to be worth a sponsor in year one: restaurants, hotels and guest houses,
pharmacies, car hire, hardware, banks, in the six or eight largest towns.

**What to build:** the slot, its label, a booking and a report. Sell the first ten by hand.

### 5. Later: data, a subscription, and sponsored guides

- **Data and alerts for firms that sell to businesses** (banks, insurers, telecoms, distributors):
  new listings by trade and town each week, an API, a file on request. One caution decides the
  shape of this. The listings are derived from OpenStreetMap under the Open Database Licence, which
  means the data itself must stay open to anyone. We can charge for the service (the API, the
  alerts, the freshness, the support) and for what we gather ourselves (claims, verifications,
  opening hours a business gives us), but we cannot sell the map's data as if it were ours. Price
  to test: MUR 5,000 to MUR 25,000 a month.
- **A subscription for accountants and founders**: reminders before each deadline in the guides
  (VAT returns, PAYE, the annual return, the registration fee), by email or WhatsApp, with the
  source of each. Price to test: MUR 300 a month for a business, more for a firm with many clients.
  The guides already hold every date; this is a calendar built on them.
- **Sponsored guides**: "This guide is kept current with the support of" one bank or one firm, a
  line at the foot, with a written rule that the sponsor sees nothing before publication. Price to
  test: MUR 40,000 a guide a year, against the MUR 30,000 a single sponsored article cost on
  lexpress.mu in 2018. Thirteen guides, and the list will grow.
- **Businesses for sale, and the ready-made brands the site began with.** A reader of "Register a
  business" is the buyer the first biznes.mu was built for. One page, linked from the guides,
  costs little and keeps that idea alive.

## What we should not do

- **Sell the order of results.** It is the one thing that cannot be bought back.
- **Run an advertising network's banners.** In a country of about 1.2 million people (Statistics
  Mauritius counts 180.5 internet subscriptions per 100 inhabitants and 2,241,100 in all) the money is
  trivial: 100,000 page views a month at one dollar a thousand, which is an assumption and a
  generous one for this market, is USD 100. It would cost speed, privacy and the look of the thing.
- **Charge to correct or remove a listing.**
- **Put the guides behind a paywall.** They are how people will find the site.
- **Sell lists of contacts.** We hold no email addresses and should keep it that way.
- **Take money from a firm to appear in a guide's text.**

## What it costs to run

- **Hosting and the crawl:** small. The whole index is a few megabytes and the crawl takes minutes.
- **Keeping the guides true:** the real cost. The rules change with every Budget and Finance Act.
  Each of the thirteen guides must be read against its sources again at least once a year, after
  the Finance Act, and whenever a body changes a fee. Allow several days each time. A wrong figure
  on a tax guide is the one thing that could hurt the name.
- **Selling:** someone has to call the first fifty firms and the first hundred businesses. No
  product on this page sells itself in year one.
- **Building:** accounts, claims, payments and counts are a few weeks of work, most of it needed
  for stream 3.

## The order to do it in

**Now, before launch (weeks 1 to 4)**
- Have a qualified person read the tax and employment guides. Close the README's confirm list.
- Choose where requests go, and launch.
- Count what matters from day one, without tracking people: searches, searches that found nothing,
  listings opened, "Call" pressed, guides read.
- Start stream 1 by hand: the agency works the list of listings with no website.

**Months 2 to 4**
- Free claiming of listings. This grows the index with data no one else has.
- The request box on the guides, with three to five partner firms a subject, sold in person.
- French pages for the guides and the directory. The search already understands a question in
  French or Kreol; the pages do not yet answer in them.

**Months 5 to 9**
- Paid profiles, once a few hundred listings are claimed.
- The first sponsored places, on the ten busiest pages, sold with a report.
- The deadline reminders.

**Months 10 to 18**
- Data and alerts for firms.
- Sponsored guides.
- More sources for the index: licensed operators, listed companies, members of associations.

## What to measure

- **Reach:** searches a day; share of searches that find nothing (and what they asked for).
- **Use:** listings opened; "Call", "Visit website" and "Directions" pressed; guides read to the end.
- **Supply:** listings claimed; listings with a telephone number; listings added by their owners.
- **Money:** requests sent to firms and the share that become clients; paid profiles and how many
  renew; revenue per thousand visits, by stream.

The searches that find nothing are the product plan. If people keep asking for plumbers and there
are four in the index, that is where the next crawl, and the next sales call, should go.

## Risks

- **No audience.** Everything waits on visitors. The guides are the best hope of being found, since
  few pages anywhere set out these rules with sources. Launch them first and keep them current.
- **A wrong figure.** See costs. Each guide already shows the day it was checked and what it could
  not confirm; keep that discipline.
- **Google Maps.** For "pharmacy near me" it will usually win. We win where it is weak: by trade
  and town in three languages, with sources, with the rules of doing business beside the listings,
  and with firms that can be asked for a quote.
- **The other directories.** Yelo states 11,344 listings and sells profiles from USD 20. We are not
  cheaper. We are more trustworthy and more useful, and should price on that.
- **The licence.** Open data keeps us honest and cheap to run, and means the raw listings can never
  be our moat. The moat is what businesses tell us themselves, the guides, and the name.
- **Trust.** One sponsored result passed off as organic, one sold contact list, and the difference
  between this and every other directory is gone.

## Decisions for the owner

1. Is the agency's own work the first stream, with the directory as its prospect list?
2. Which partner firms would we approach first for introductions, and on what terms?
3. Is MUR 4,800 a year the right opening price for a verified profile?
4. Who re-reads the guides after each Finance Act, and who signs them off?
5. May we count visits in a way that respects privacy, so the next version of this page has
   measurements in place of assumptions?
